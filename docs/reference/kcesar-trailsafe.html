While you wait

King County SAR resources are requested through 911 and King County dispatch — not through this app.
Call 911 Text 911
STOPStop moving. Stay calm. You will think more clearly standing still than while wandering.
  1. Stop moving and stay calm.
  2. Think: when did you last know exactly where you were?
  3. Observe your surroundings, resources, weather, daylight, and hazards.
  4. Plan — don't make the situation worse.
If you are genuinely lost, unsafe, or don't know how to get back safelyCall 911. Do not wait for darkness or worsening weather.

If you're already in contact with 911 or SAR

Stay where you are unless a dispatcher or rescuer tells you to move, or you must move a short distance to reach immediate safety.

Additional actions

  • Get out of any immediate hazard.
  • Don't assume a creek or drainage leads safely downhill to a road or town. In Cascade terrain, drainages can lead into waterfalls, cliffs, canyons, and dense brush. If you're genuinely lost, stop and call rather than following this common myth.
  • Keep your party together.
  • Put on insulation before you get cold, not after.
  • Prepare shelter if conditions warrant it.
  • Make yourself visible.
  • Preserve your phone battery.
  • Answer calls or texts from unknown numbers while a rescue is active.
  • Follow dispatcher or rescuer instructions over anything in this app.
Call 911 Text 911
  1. Make the scene safe so there aren't more victims.
  2. Address immediate life threats using the first-aid skills you actually have.
  3. If the person can't safely self-rescue, has a serious injury or illness, or you're unsure — call 911 early.
  4. Give an exact location and describe the patient's condition.
  5. Protect the person from cold, wet ground, wind, rain, or heat.
  6. Stay available to 911 and follow their instructions.
Inability to walk, inability to safely self-rescue, a serious medical problem, or being unable to make reasonable progress toward the trailhead are all good reasons to call 911.

This app doesn't try to diagnose or treat medical conditions. For anything beyond basic first aid, defer to your training and to 911.

Call 911 Text 911

Exhaustion, terrain beyond your ability, gear failure, darkness, or impassable snow or water can all leave you unable to safely continue.

  1. Stop before exhaustion or darkness turns this into an injury.
  2. Move only as much as necessary to reach a safe location.
  3. Evaluate daylight, weather, clothing, shelter, food, water, and communications.
  4. If you can't safely self-rescue, or you're unsure — call 911.
  5. Prepare to stay warm, stay dry, and wait.
Call before it becomes catastrophicIt's normal — and expected — to call for help before a manageable problem turns into an emergency.

If separation just occurred

  • Stop the rest of the group from scattering.
  • Identify the last place and time everyone was together.
  • Try normal contact methods.
  • Don't send multiple uncoordinated people off searching in different directions.

Call 911 when

  • The missing person can't quickly be located or contacted.
  • They're a child, an at-risk adult, injured, or inadequately equipped.
  • Darkness, weather, or terrain raise the urgency.

Have this ready for 911

  • Name and age
  • Clothing and pack description
  • Medical considerations
  • Phone or device number
  • Last known point and intended route
  • Experience level and footwear, if useful
Call 911 Text 911

Is the person overdue and you can't reach them?

  1. Try the phone or contact method on their trip plan.
  2. Review the intended return time and the "if you have not heard from me by" time.
  3. Gather their complete trip plan.
  4. If they remain overdue and can't be contacted — call 911.
  5. Tell 911 they're overdue from an outdoor trip and provide names, party size, planned route, trailhead, timing, vehicle, clothing, communications devices, and any medical or experience information you know.
An overdue outdoor traveler who can't be contacted is a legitimate reason to call 911 — you don't need to wait longer to be sure.
Call 911 Text 911
  1. If contacting 911, give your location and the type of help needed first.
  2. Stay available to answer follow-up questions.
  3. Follow dispatcher or responder instructions over anything in this app.
  4. Move only as needed for immediate safety.

Once you've called for help, this is what actually helps.

  • Follow the 911 call taker's or rescuer's instructions over anything in this app.
  • Stay where rescuers expect you unless staying is immediately unsafe.
  • Keep your party together.
  • Prevent further injury.
  • Get warm and dry early — don't wait until you feel cold.
  • Make yourself visible.
  • Keep communication devices accessible.

Preserve your battery

  • Turn on Low Power Mode — it preserves calls and messages while reducing other power use.
  • Reduce screen brightness and let the screen sleep when you're not using it.
  • Stop optional high-power activities like recording video.
  • Don't force-close apps as a generic battery-saving step.
  • Don't blindly disable cellular, Wi-Fi, or Bluetooth — those may be needed for a 911 callback, messaging, or a paired satellite device. Only turn off what you know is unnecessary.

Help rescuers find you

  • Don't repeatedly call 911 for status updates unless instructed or the situation changes.
  • Be ready to describe nearby landmarks, sounds, terrain, trail signs, water, roads, or other features.
  • If you hear or see searchers or aircraft, signal in a controlled, obvious way.
  • Never approach an aircraft unless a crew member directs you to.

    About KCESAR

    King County Explorer Search & Rescue (KCESAR) is an operational search-and-rescue unit and a separate 501(c)(3) nonprofit. It describes itself as the largest member unit of King County Search & Rescue and King County's primary ground search-and-rescue team.

    kcesar.org →

    About King County Search & Rescue

    The King County Search & Rescue Association (KCSARA, often shortened to KCSAR) is the non-operational umbrella association that represents and supports King County's specialized SAR member units, including KCESAR. Member units keep their own identities — an official KCESAR app isn't automatically a KCSARA or King County Sheriff's Office app.

    kingcountysar.org →

    Important

    This app is not monitored Nobody at KCESAR, KCSARA, or anywhere else is watching your location or trip plans. TrailSafe doesn't alert anyone automatically. In an emergency, always call or text 911.

    Privacy

    Your trip plans and profile stay on your device. KCESAR does not receive or monitor your location or trip information. Information leaves the app only when you explicitly share it using your phone.

    • No account, ever
    • No analytics or advertising SDKs
    • No cloud database or background location
    • Delete any or all saved plans at any time

    Timing

    Expected return vs. overdue time These are different on purpose. Expected return is when you think you'll be back. "If you have not heard from me by" is when your contact should treat this as genuinely overdue and start the steps below.

    Add enough time for an ordinary delay — slow travel, photos, traffic, a minor route change — without waiting so long that darkness, weather, cold, or a medical problem could get much worse. There's no universal rule; choose this time on purpose.
